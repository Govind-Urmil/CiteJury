CiteJury Authority Guide

Legal Citation Guide for Law Students

Students usually lose marks on citation not because the topic is hard, but because source details, pinpoints, and required styles are not checked carefully.

Trust-first note: CiteJury is a drafting and learning tool. Important citations should be verified against the original authority and the required style guide before submission.

A simple student workflow

Save the source PDF or official page.

Record the citation details before you start writing.

Use CiteJury to create a structured draft.

Verify the final citation against your teacher, university, journal, or moot rulebook.

Common mistakes

Using only a case name without reporter, court, date, or neutral citation.

Forgetting pinpoint pages or paragraphs for quotations.

Mixing OSCOLA, Bluebook, Indian reporter style, and university house style in one document.

Copying citations from blogs or notes without checking the original source.

Good habits

Create a source table for every assignment.

Keep one citation style consistently unless instructed otherwise.

Mark uncertain citations for manual verification before submission.

Examples and checks

Assignment habit

Draft first, verify before final submission.

Memorial habit

Follow the moot proposition and rulebook over generic guides.

Educational use only.

This guide explains citation structure and verification habits. It is not legal advice and does not replace a court, journal, university, publisher, or competition citation rule.
