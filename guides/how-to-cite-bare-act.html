How to Cite a Bare Act or Statute

A statute citation is usually simpler than a case citation, but precision is essential because one word, clause, explanation, or proviso can change the legal point.

Trust-first note: CiteJury is a drafting and learning tool. Important citations should be verified against the original authority and the required style guide before submission.

Basic structure

Use the exact short title of the Act.

Include the year of enactment.

Add the section, sub-section, clause, proviso, explanation, schedule, rule, or regulation relied on.

Pinpointing provisions

Use “s.” for a section only if your required style accepts that abbreviation.

For complex provisions, include nested references, for example section 2(1)(d) or article 19(1)(a).

For delegated legislation, identify the rules/regulations and the parent Act if required.

Verification

Check India Code, the official Gazette, the current bare act, or an authoritative source for amendments.

Be careful with repealed, amended, or substituted provisions. A citation can be formally neat but legally outdated.

Examples and checks

Simple statute

Indian Evidence Act, 1872, s. 3.

Nested provision

Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, Order VII r. 11.

Educational use only.

This guide explains citation structure and verification habits. It is not legal advice and does not replace a court, journal, university, publisher, or competition citation rule.
