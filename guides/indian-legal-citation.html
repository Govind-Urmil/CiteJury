Indian Legal Citation Guide

Indian legal writing often combines official court information, recognised law reports, statutory titles, and institutional style requirements. CiteJury keeps these categories separate so unsupported formats are not overstated.

Trust-first note: CiteJury is a drafting and learning tool. Important citations should be verified against the original authority and the required style guide before submission.

Indian case citations

A reliable case citation should identify the parties, year, court, reporter or neutral citation, and pinpoint where necessary.

For Supreme Court of India neutral citations, CiteJury supports the official YYYYINSCN token format only within the documented scope.

For SCC/AIR-style reporter citations, the tool formats common components but still recommends checking the reporter source and institutional requirement.

Indian legislation citations

Use the exact short title and year of the Act. Add the section, sub-section, clause, explanation, schedule, or article relied on.

For bare acts and statutes, do not invent a publisher or reporter unless your style guide specifically requires it.

Verification habits

Use official court websites, e-SCR/Supreme Court sources, India Code, Gazette/publication sources, or the relevant law report where possible.

For moot courts and universities, follow the competition or institution rulebook even if a general citation guide differs.
