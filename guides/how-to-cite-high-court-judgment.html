How to Cite a High Court Judgment

High Court judgments can have similar party names across different states, so court identification and source clarity matter.

Trust-first note: CiteJury is a drafting and learning tool. Important citations should be verified against the original authority and the required style guide before submission.

Core details

Party names exactly as shown.

Court name or abbreviation, such as Delhi High Court, Bombay High Court, Madras High Court, etc.

Neutral citation if the relevant High Court uses one, or reporter citation where available.

Decision year and pinpoint paragraph/page if needed.

Avoid ambiguity

Do not omit the court when the citation could be confused with another jurisdiction.

Do not assume every High Court uses the same neutral citation pattern. CiteJury currently treats High Court neutral citations cautiously unless a specific rule is documented.

Verification

Check the High Court website, official order copy, recognised reporter, or required institutional style.

For case-law propositions, verify the paragraph you cite, not just the citation header.

Examples and checks

High Court citation checklist

Case name + court + year + neutral/reporter citation + pinpoint.

When reporter is missing

Use clear court and date information, then manually verify style requirements.

Educational use only.

This guide explains citation structure and verification habits. It is not legal advice and does not replace a court, journal, university, publisher, or competition citation rule.
