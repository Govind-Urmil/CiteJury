How to Cite a Legal Website

Web citations are fragile because pages move, update, or disappear. A good legal web citation records enough detail for a reader to identify the page you used.

Trust-first note: CiteJury is a drafting and learning tool. Important citations should be verified against the original authority and the required style guide before submission.

Collect these details

Page title.

Website or publisher name.

Author or institution if shown.

Publication or last-updated date if available.

URL and access date if required.

Authority check

Prefer official court, government, university, regulator, publisher, or journal pages.

Avoid relying on unattributed summaries for important legal propositions.

Archive or save a copy when your institution permits it and the source is important.

CiteJury scope

CiteJury can format basic legal website citations from supplied fields.

It cannot verify whether the webpage is current, official, or legally authoritative.

Examples and checks

Website checklist

Title + website/publisher + date + URL + access date.

Verification note

Check whether the page is official and current.

Educational use only.

This guide explains citation structure and verification habits. It is not legal advice and does not replace a court, journal, university, publisher, or competition citation rule.
