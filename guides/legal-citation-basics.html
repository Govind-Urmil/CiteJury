Legal Citation Basics

Legal citation is not decoration. It is a compact address that helps a reader identify, verify, and evaluate the authority used in legal writing.

Trust-first note: CiteJury is a drafting and learning tool. Important citations should be verified against the original authority and the required style guide before submission.

What a legal citation must do

Identify the authority: case, statute, article, book, website, or other source.

Identify where it can be found: reporter, neutral citation, publisher, journal, URL, or official database.

Identify the exact point relied on: page, paragraph, article, section, clause, or schedule.

Signal the applicable citation style: court practice, university rule, journal guide, OSCOLA, Bluebook, AGLC, or another system.

Core components to collect before formatting

Party names or title exactly as shown in the source.

Year of judgment, publication, enactment, or access.

Reporter, neutral citation, volume, page, court, journal, publisher, or URL where relevant.

Pinpoint reference if you rely on a specific page, paragraph, section, article, or clause.

Accuracy checklist

Prefer the official court, legislation, journal, publisher, or style-guide source where available.

Do not cite from memory; copy names, dates, reporter details, and pinpoints from the source.

Check whether your institution wants footnotes, bibliography entries, or both.

When CiteJury says manual verification is recommended, treat the generated citation as a structured draft, not a final authority.

Examples and checks

Case citation component check

Party names + year + reporter or neutral citation + court if needed + pinpoint.

Statute citation component check

Short title + year + section/sub-section/clause/schedule.

Educational use only.

This guide explains citation structure and verification habits. It is not legal advice and does not replace a court, journal, university, publisher, or competition citation rule.
