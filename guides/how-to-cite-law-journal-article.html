How to Cite a Law Journal Article

Journal article citations should let a reader identify the author, article title, journal, publication year, and exact page relied on.

Trust-first note: CiteJury is a drafting and learning tool. Important citations should be verified against the original authority and the required style guide before submission.

Collect these details

Author name as published.

Article title.

Journal name, volume, issue if required, year, and starting page.

Pinpoint page for a specific argument or quotation.

DOI or stable URL if your style guide requires it.

Style differences

OSCOLA, Bluebook, AGLC, and journal house styles handle article titles, italics, punctuation, and pinpoints differently.

Do not assume one article format works for all institutions.

Verification

Check the journal PDF or official page, not only a database snippet.

Confirm page range and pinpoint before final submission.

Examples and checks

Article checklist

Author + title + journal + volume/year + page + pinpoint.

Online article addition

Add DOI/stable URL/access date only if required by the style.

Educational use only.

This guide explains citation structure and verification habits. It is not legal advice and does not replace a court, journal, university, publisher, or competition citation rule.
