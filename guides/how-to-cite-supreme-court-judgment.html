How to Cite a Supreme Court Judgment

A Supreme Court judgment citation should help the reader find the exact decision and the exact passage you rely on.

Trust-first note: CiteJury is a drafting and learning tool. Important citations should be verified against the original authority and the required style guide before submission.

Collect these details first

Case name as shown in the judgment or reporter.

Year of pronouncement or reporter year.

Neutral citation if available, especially for recent Supreme Court materials.

Reporter citation such as SCC, SCR, AIR, or another recognised report if you are using one.

Pinpoint paragraph or page if you rely on a specific proposition.

Neutral citation scope

CiteJury supports the Supreme Court of India neutral citation token pattern documented by the Court: year + INSC + serial number.

The tool validates the token shape, but it does not verify that the serial number belongs to a real judgment. Final verification should be done against the Supreme Court source or reliable database.

Practical workflow

Generate a draft citation.

Open the original judgment or reporter.

Verify party names, year, court, neutral citation/reporter citation, and pinpoint.

Follow the court, journal, university, or moot rulebook if it gives a specific format.
